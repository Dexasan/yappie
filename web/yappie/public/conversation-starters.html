Conversation starters for chatting with strangers

Start with a question someone can answer without giving away personal details.

A first text chat does not need an impressive opening line. Pick something you are curious about, give a little of your own answer, and leave room for the other person to respond. These questions work with the optional interests on Yappie, or in any first conversation.

Music

  • What song have you played more than once this week?
  • Would you rather see your favourite artist in a tiny venue or at a festival?
  • What album took a second listen before you liked it?

Follow up: “What do you like about it?” is more useful than immediately asking for another recommendation. Share a song of your own so the conversation feels like an exchange.

Gaming

  • What game would you happily play again for the first time?
  • Do you prefer cooperative games, competitive games, or playing on your own?
  • Which game has the best soundtrack?

Follow up: Ask about a moment they remember from the game. You do not need their gamer tag, account name, or a link to keep talking.

Movies and anime

  • What film or series surprised you recently?
  • Is there a fictional world you would like to visit for a day?
  • Do you prefer watching something new or rewatching a favourite?

Follow up: Check whether they have seen something before sharing a spoiler. If you disagree about a show, ask what worked for them instead of turning it into a debate.

Travel and everyday life

  • Would your ideal day out involve a city, the countryside, or the sea?
  • What food would you recommend to someone trying it for the first time?
  • What is a small thing that made today better?

Follow up: Talk about preferences and experiences. Avoid asking for a current location, address, school, workplace, or exact daily routine.

Deeper conversations

  • What is something you changed your mind about recently?
  • If you had a month to learn one skill, what would you choose?
  • What makes a conversation feel worthwhile to you?

Follow up: Let the other person decide how much to share. A stranger does not owe you a personal story. Change the subject if a question feels too private.

Language practice

Yappie lets you choose a chat language, but it does not verify fluency or provide a language teacher. Ask whether the other person wants to practise before turning the conversation into a lesson.

  • Would you like to practise this language together?
  • Would you prefer corrections as we go, or just a relaxed conversation?
  • Can you explain a favourite food or hobby in a few sentences?

When the conversation stalls

Try one follow-up about their last answer, then offer something of your own. A string of unrelated questions can feel like an interview. If the other person stops replying or wants to leave, let the conversation end.

You can say “Thanks for the chat, I’m heading off” without giving a reason. If someone is abusive or pressures you for personal details, leave and use the report or block controls. Read the chat safety guide for more.
